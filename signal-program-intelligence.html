01 — The TPM problem

The information usually exists. The problem is connecting it.

A formal date lives in a ticket. A newer forecast appears in a conversation. A dependency is mentioned in a meeting. The launch plan lives somewhere else. The TPM becomes the integration layer.

“We're probably not going to make Oct 26; it may be mid-November” is where semantic interpretation adds value.

Three hard problems

Contradictory evidence

A formal commitment and a newer forecast can both be true.

Permissions & trust

Agents should retrieve only evidence the user is authorized to see.

Adoption

It must save TPM attention, not add another place to maintain.

05 — What I learned

The biggest opportunity for AI in program management isn't another status report.

It's helping TPMs connect fragmented signals, see what materially changed, and focus human judgment where it matters most. AI doesn't replace the TPM; it moves their time from collecting status to making better decisions.

Agentforce could provide the agent and orchestration layer; SignalPath is the TPM-specific intelligence pattern. Before any of it is real: read-only connectors, a shared audit-trailed backend, identity and permissions, and cost and security controls.

An implementation direction, not a claim that the prototype is deployed in or connected to Salesforce systems.
